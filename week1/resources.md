# Week 1 resources — Information is surprise

Total: about 3–4 hours. Watch and read in this order, then open `starter.ipynb`.

| # | Resource | Time | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Solving Wordle using information theory — 3Blue1Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v68zYyaEmEA) | 30 min | The best intuition for "information = surprise", using a game you know |
| 2 | [Visual Information Theory — Chris Olah](https://colah.github.io/posts/2015-09-Visual-Information/) | 60 min | Entropy, cross-entropy and KL divergence, drawn. Read it twice; it is the foundation for the whole month |
| 3 | [Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms — David MacKay](https://www.inference.org.uk/itila/book.html), Ch. 4–5 (free PDF) | 90 min | The source coding theorem and symbol codes, including Huffman. Skim the proofs, read the examples |
| 4 | [A Mathematical Theory of Communication — Claude Shannon (1948)](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf), Sections 1–7 | Optional | The original paper. Surprisingly readable |

## Key ideas to be able to explain after this week

- Why an event with probability *p* carries −log₂ *p* bits
- What entropy is, and what it is a lower bound *for* (and for what it isn't)
- Why gzip can beat order-0 entropy on English
- How Huffman builds a prefix-free code, and its one big weakness
